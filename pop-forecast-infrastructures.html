    About these figures

    This table reports verified major infrastructure only. A project is included on a region tab solely if it has a verifiable estimated cost, start year and end year, and is a confirmed major project (~$50M+) located within the named region. Completed, cancelled and uncertain projects are excluded.

    Inclusion rules — a project appears on a region tab only if ALL of the following are true

    • Cost, start year AND end year are all verifiable from a credible source.
    • It is a confirmed major project of roughly $50 million or more.
    • It is physically located within the named region (not state/territory-wide).
    • It is not completed, cancelled or merely speculative/unconfirmed.

    Construction jobs are always shown — the published figure where one exists, otherwise a sector-based estimate (jobs per $1B).

    Why some regions are lower than earlier versions

    Earlier versions counted projects broadly — including small works, state/territory-wide programs and unconfirmed proposals. This version applies one consistent standard, so some regions total less. It reflects stricter measurement, not a decline in activity, and is not directly comparable to the earlier broad lists.

    Why this version is easier to maintain

    Every quarter, the same rule decides which projects count. So when a region's total goes up or down, it's because projects were genuinely added, finished, or changed — not because we counted differently this time.

    The old version had no set rule, so a total could change just because the list was put together differently. With a fixed rule, the numbers stay consistent and you can compare one quarter to the next with confidence.

    About this version

    This is the earlier, broader version of the dataset. It was compiled before a consistent inclusion rule was applied, so it covers a much wider range of projects — and the totals are correspondingly higher.

    What it includes

    • Projects of all sizes, including small council works well under the $50M threshold used later.
    • State- and territory-wide programs counted against a single region — for example, NT-wide road, rail and energy projects all sit under Darwin, which is the main reason Darwin's numbers look so high here.
    • Proposed, speculative and unconfirmed projects that may not have firm costs or timelines.
    • Some projects without a verified cost, start year or end year.

    Why the numbers are higher

    Because the list was assembled broadly rather than against a fixed standard, it captures far more projects than the verified June 2026 version. The higher spend and job figures reflect that wider, looser scope — not greater real activity. Treat these figures as an inclusive long-list, and use the June 2026 version for like-for-like, verified comparisons.
